# Qualification and lifecycle

## Qualification score

Score each dimension from 0–5 and record evidence or `Unknown`: problem severity, buyer clarity, frequency, strategic fit, repeatability, technical feasibility, delivery feasibility, data availability, quality risk, security risk, margin potential, recurring-revenue potential, differentiation, and evidence strength.

Risk dimensions are readiness scores: 5 means controlled low risk; 0 means unacceptable or unknown high risk. Calculate percentage as `sum / 70 × 100`. A score is directional, never a substitute for evidence or approval.

Use these recommendations:

- Explore: incomplete problem or buyer evidence.
- Validate: plausible problem with testable high-risk assumptions.
- Pilot: bounded hypothesis, baseline, criteria, owner, and approvals exist.
- Productize: repeatable outcome demonstrated with delivery, quality, and economics evidence.
- Scale: active product has repeatable adoption, controlled variation, support capacity, and approved economics.
- Hold: dependency, risk, evidence, or capacity gap prevents safe progress.
- Retire: strategic fit, demand, economics, quality, or support evidence no longer justifies continuation.

## Lifecycle gates

Allowed lifecycle sequence is `Idea → Discovery → Validation → Pilot → Active → Scaling → Sunset → Retired`. Hold is a concept recommendation, not a lifecycle shortcut.

- Discovery requires problem, audience, source evidence, owner, and correlation ID.
- Validation requires scored qualification, riskiest assumptions, experiment plan, baseline, and success/failure criteria.
- Pilot requires human approval, bounded scope, acceptance criteria, QA/security review plan, and economics inputs.
- Active requires successful pilot evidence, measured client outcome, actual effort and defects, approved current version, support model, and approval decision.
- Scaling requires adoption, delivery-variation, quality, capacity, recurring-revenue, and margin evidence across more than one anecdote.
- Sunset requires impact analysis, communication plan, successor or exit path, and approval.
- Retired requires effective date, archived versions, proposal exclusion, and retained decision evidence.

Never skip a stage. Promotion requires an approval decision and evidence references. Demotion is allowed when evidence deteriorates. A superseded or retired version is never proposal-eligible.
