# Symbiont Operational Maturity Report and 90-Day Roadmap

**Prepared by:** COO Agent 001  
**Date:** 2026-07-18  
**Assessment basis:** Foundational charter and stated software stack; no live operational data, contracts, financials, client records, or team interviews were provided. All maturity ratings are therefore provisional assumptions to validate in Week 1.

## Executive Summary

Symbiont has a strong, differentiated vision and a clear AI-native operating philosophy. Its principal constraint is not ambition; it is the absence of verified, integrated operating evidence. The immediate priority is to establish source-of-truth discipline, controlled delivery, and reliable management data before scaling agents or automations.

This package creates the v1 operating backbone. The next 90 days should prove it on live work, instrument the company, and automate only stable workflows. The expected result is faster proposals, more predictable delivery, stronger client confidence, reduced administrative load, and reusable intellectual property.

## Key Risks

| Priority | Risk | Consequence | Immediate response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Automating undefined or unreliable processes | Faster errors and hidden rework | Pilot SOPs and baseline metrics first |
| Critical | AI authority exceeds governance | Client, legal, privacy, financial exposure | Default L1; explicit approvals and audit logs |
| High | Multiple systems become competing sources | Conflicting status and low trust | Publish system-of-record map and integrate by ID |
| High | Scope and acceptance remain implicit | Margin loss, disputes, rework | Contract traceability and change control |
| High | QA/QC varies by person | Defects and reputational damage | Independent release gate and evidence |
| High | Financial/project data are not joined | Late margin/cash surprises | Project IDs across CRM, ClickUp, QuickBooks, BI |
| High | Knowledge capture becomes storage, not reuse | Search noise and stale guidance | Ownership, lifecycle, review score, archive |
| Medium | Tool stack creates overhead | Cost and fragmented behavior | Application inventory and integration rules |
| Medium | Over-customized services resist scale | Low margin and limited IP | Productize repeated scope modules and data schemas |
| Medium | Security/data rights unclear | Client trust and compliance exposure | Classification, least privilege, data lineage |

## 90-Day Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Control and Visibility (Days 1–30)

**Outcome:** One operating truth and controlled live work.

- Founder approves operating system, authority matrix, and owners.
- Populate all registers and migrate/link active work.
- Validate the ten SOPs on at least one real workflow each; revise to v1.0.
- Implement project baseline, weekly portfolio review, QA/QC, and decision cadence.
- Define KPIs and publish dashboard v0.1 with source freshness and data-quality score.
- Charter only the initial eight agents; keep new workflow agents at L1.
- Deploy Wave 1 low-risk automations.
- Complete security/data classification and software access review.

**Exit criteria:** 100% active projects/opportunities registered; every critical task has owner/date; every issued deliverable has QA evidence; dashboard figures reconcile to sources; no agent lacks owner/charter.

### Phase 2 — Integration and Predictability (Days 31–60)

**Outcome:** Faster handoffs and reliable forecasts.

- Connect CRM, ClickUp, QuickBooks, QA, and project registers by stable IDs.
- Automate project setup, meeting capture, status aggregation, and proposal assembly drafts.
- Build standard product/scope modules with effort, price, acceptance, and risk assumptions.
- Track forecast accuracy, first-pass QA yield, proposal cycle, stage conversion, DSO, and margin.
- Run monthly process review; remove duplicated data entry and unused fields.
- Create client experience checkpoints: intake, kickoff, milestone, closeout, benefits review.

**Exit criteria:** proposal median <2 business days; weekly forecasts submitted on time; portfolio refresh daily; >90% required CRM/project fields complete; repeated manual data entry reduced by 20%.

### Phase 3 — Productization and Learning (Days 61–90)

**Outcome:** Operations generate reusable IP and measurable leverage.

- Deploy Wave 3 automations under release gates.
- Convert repeated deliverables, data schemas, dashboards, and workflows into product modules.
- Establish benefits-realization reviews and case-study pipeline with permission controls.
- Use defect, risk, win/loss, and project margin data to revise pricing and standards.
- Recertify agents, permissions, evaluations, ROI, and incident history.
- Publish roadmap v2 focused on recurring revenue and differentiated building-intelligence products.

**Exit criteria:** >=30% validated repetitive-work reduction; >=80% recurring workflows documented; 95% decisions logged in one day; 90% milestones on time; measurable recurring-revenue pipeline; at least three reusable product/IP modules.

## Recommendations

### P0 — Adopt governance baseline

**Effort:** Low, 1–2 days.  
**Expected ROI:** Immediate reduction in ambiguity and execution risk.  
**Decision:** Approve owners, systems of record, authority levels, and mandatory controls.

### P1 — Instrument live work

**Effort:** Medium, 1–2 weeks.  
**Expected ROI:** Earlier visibility into margin, schedule, cash, client risk, and capacity.  
**Decision:** Require IDs and minimum data on all active work.

### P1 — Productize before scaling delivery

**Effort:** Medium-high, 30–90 days.  
**Expected ROI:** Faster proposals/delivery, stronger margins, more recurring revenue and defensible IP.  
**Decision:** Select the three most repeated client outcomes for product modules.

### P1 — Keep agents bounded and observable

**Effort:** Medium, ongoing.  
**Expected ROI:** AI leverage without uncontrolled external, financial, contractual, or data exposure.  
**Decision:** Default to L1, promote only after evidence.

## Expected Return

The near-term return is confidence: fewer missed commitments, faster decision-making, traceable quality, and reliable forecasts. The structural return is leverage: knowledge becomes controlled IP, proposals become configurations, delivery becomes a repeatable product system, and AI scales proven operations instead of improvising them.
